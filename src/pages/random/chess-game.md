This game comes from the first ever over-the-board tournament I played, in January 2020. It was game seven of nine in a five-day all-play-all, two games a day with one bye. Time control was 75 minutes plus 30 seconds a move, and this one lasted almost three hours.

**White:** Damian Evans · **Black:** Ciaran O'Reilly · **Result:** 1–0 · Caro-Kann Defence, Two Knights Attack

---

I now play 1. d4, but at the time it was 1. e4 every game. My theory was next to nothing, but I like to think I could avoid most early traps in the Italian and Spanish.

**1. e4 c6 2. Nf3 d5 3. Nc3 Bg4 4. d3** I had no idea how to play against the Caro-Kann, and d3 certainly isn't an aggressive response. My plan was just to support e4 and get a solid centre.

**4... e6 5. a3** To avoid an annoying Bb4.

**5... Nf6 6. Be2 Be7 7. h3 Bxf3 8. Bxf3 Qc7 9. O-O O-O 10. Ne2** From here on, the position had never been seen on Lichess.

**10... Nbd7 11. b3 Rfd8 12. Bb2?! Ne5** Bb2 looks fine, but it allows 12... dxe4, winning a pawn (13. dxe4 Nc5 14. Qc1 Nfxe4).

**13. Bxe5 Qxe5 14. d4 Qc7 15. e5 Nd7** The engine says the game is even, but it feels comfortable for White, to me at least.

**16. Qd3 c5 17. c3 Rdc8 18. Bg4 c4 19. bxc4 Qxc4 20. Qg3 Rc6 21. Rfc1** Attacking straight away with f4 would have been better than defending.

**21... f6? 22. Nf4** At this point I was choosing between Nf4 and exf6. After maybe ten minutes I decided on exf6, and my hand promptly came out and played Nf4. I couldn't believe it. I muttered some frustrated words and left the room for a minute to swear, thinking I'd thrown it away. When I came back Black hadn't moved, and when I looked again I realised everything was still fine. Going through the game with an engine that night, this was where I got the advantage: exf6 Bxf6 wouldn't have given me anything, while after Nf4 the engine gives White over +2, though it's a complicated position.

**22... f5?** My unintended display of frustration may have put my opponent off. He told me afterwards that while I was out of the room he spent a while looking for the blunder I seemed to think I'd made. Either way, f5 is a mistake, because it allows Be2. Black's queen then has nowhere to go but a4, which sets up a possible Bb5 forking queen and rook. Otherwise Black has to allow Rb7 and complete penetration into his position.

**23. Be2 Qa4 24. Rcb1 a6** Apparently 24... Rb6 is best, but after 25. Nxe6 (threatening mate on g7) Rxe6 26. Bb5 Qa5 27. Bxd7 White wins a piece.

**25. Rxb7 Rd8 26. Rc7 Bf8** Protecting g7, since 26... Rxc7 loses to 27. Nxe6.

**27. Rb7 Qa5** Black's position is falling apart. g7 is under heavy pressure, the light-squared bishop is eyeing the a2-g8 diagonal, and almost every Black move seems to weaken something.

**28. c4 Nxe5** Black gives up the knight to try to push the attack back. 28... Rb6 would have held better.

**29. cxd5** dxe5 would have been better, because of Rc3.

**29... exd5 30. dxe5 g6 31. Qh4 h5 32. Bxh5** Everyone loves a bishop sacrifice, right?

**32... gxh5** And it's mate in seven.

**33. Qg5+ Kh8 34. Ng6+ Kg8 35. Ne7+ Kf7** Black resigned here, but it could have finished **36. Qxh5+ Kg7 37. Nxf5+ Kg8 38. Qh7#**.

---

My coach [Marko Makaj](https://lichess.org/coach/MarkoMakaj) called this (especially after Nf4) at least a 2100 game. I got lucky with my hand playing a different move from the one I'd chosen, but I converted the advantage well and was really pleased with it. Ciaran and I had a great post-mortem too. My best over-the-board game up to that date.
